# Border Drift: project brief

The single source of truth for what we're building and why. Both owners and both Claudes read this before starting work. Update it in the same PR whenever a decision changes.

## The goal
**Make the best true-size map on the internet, with history and games built in.** Quality comes first. A portfolio piece and a possible business are welcome side effects, not the main objective, so we don't cut corners for speed or monetization.

- **Name:** Border Drift (Oct 21, chosen by Anthony and Evan; "Scale Shift" until then). The name lives in `src/content/site.ts`. The repo, the Vercel project and the URL stay `scale-shift` until the domain is bought (borderdrift.com, see the launch checklist in issue #40).
- **Owners:** Anthony (Mac) and Evan (Windows). Shared ownership of everything.
- **Repo:** https://github.com/AnthonyTFM/scale-shift (public). Hosting: Vercel, auto-deploys `main`.
- **Inspiration:** thetruesize.com. We rebuilt the idea with original code, copy, name and design, and added 4,000 years of history and a daily game. Never copy its text, branding or layout.

On Oct 19, 2026 the site was rebuilt from the "Atlas Drift" prototype (the v1 code is in git history before PR #3).

## How it works (key decisions, don't undo without discussing)
- **No map tiles.** The map is drawn on canvas by d3-geo from our own data: no API keys or per-view costs.
- **Real-size math:** shapes are rotated on the sphere (`src/lib/geo.ts`), then projected. Area never changes. Never move shapes by flat pixel offsets.
- **Data pipeline:** `npm run data` turns historical-basemaps (GPL-3.0), Cliopatria (CC BY 4.0: the base from 1800 to 1960, and fixes before 1800), HistoGIS, OpenHistoricalMap and Natural Earth (public domain) into TopoJSON per snapshot in `public/data/eras/`, with fixes from `src/content/data-corrections.json`, then builds the Antarctic claims and the daily puzzle pool. `npm run check:data` guards it in CI.
- **Accurate borders from 1900 (Oct 25, Evan):** the maps from 1900 on are cross-checked against CShapes 2.0 (ETH Zurich, the academic standard for borders since 1886) and corrected. CShapes is CC BY-NC-SA, so it is a reference only: it is never shipped or traced ("facts in, drawings out"), and every shipped outline comes from an open source. Europe in 1900 and 1914 comes from HistoGIS (CC BY 4.0, `scripts/lib/histogis.mjs`), Europe from 1920 to 1960 from OpenHistoricalMap (CC0, `scripts/lib/ohm.mjs`, dated relations listed by id in `OHM` in `build-data.mjs`), 1994–2010 from Natural Earth rolled back, and land drawn for the wrong country anywhere else is given back using Natural Earth's countries and provinces (`TERRITORY` in `build-data.mjs`; 1900 Africa is redrawn that way as it was divided between colonies, keeping the states that still ruled themselves). Where a new outline replaces the source's, the thin pieces of the old shape left along it are removed (land ones go to the neighbours through the coast fill). Evan reviews these 100 years at a time: 1900 to today first, then 1800–1900 (below). Accepted gaps are listed under Known issues.
- **1800–1880 without historical-basemaps (Oct 25, Evan):** Evan asked that the 1800, 1815, 1878 and 1880 maps use none of André Ourednik's data. They are built from Cliopatria v0.2.1 (Seshat Global History Databank, CC BY 4.0, `scripts/lib/cliopatria.mjs`: the world's polities digitised from historical atlases), with OpenHistoricalMap's dated borders laid over it (`scripts/ohm-1800s.json`: relation ids per map with our name, ruler, note and the Cliopatria polity each one replaces, plus the relations left out and why) and HistoGIS in Europe from 1815 (`HISTOGIS` in `build-data.mjs`). Where one source keeps a single outline for decades while the borders moved (OpenHistoricalMap's Awadh 1722–1801 and Maratha Confederacy 1788–1818), the other source's dated outline is used. Cliopatria's names and rulers are fixed in `data-corrections.json` like the old source's; places neither source draws right are set in `TERRITORY` (Natural Earth outlines, Cliopatria parts, or an approximate outline marked as such). CShapes starts in 1886, so these maps are checked place by place against the sources and the record (31 December of each year).
- **1900–1960 without historical-basemaps (Oct 25, Evan):** the 1900, 1914, 1920, 1930, 1938, 1945 and 1960 maps are built the same way, so no map from 1800 to 1960 uses André Ourednik's data. Cliopatria is the base, cut to Natural Earth's coastline (on every Cliopatria map, so no strip of its outline is left at sea where a better outline takes the land), with OpenHistoricalMap's countries laid over it (`scripts/ohm-1900s.json`, generated: a relation that is the same land as a shape on the reviewed maps of PR #136 takes that shape's name, ruler and note unless the name came later; territories OpenHistoricalMap files below country level, like Papua, the Far Eastern Republic and French Indochina's protectorates, are added by hand), then the hand-checked European lists (1920–1960) and HistoGIS (1900, 1914), then the `TERRITORY` rules. The corrections keyed to the old source's names were replaced by ones for Cliopatria's. Checked place by place (380 places), shape by shape against the reviewed maps, and against CShapes, which they match better than the reviewed maps on every date.
- **Before 1800 checked against Cliopatria (Oct 26, Evan asked to cross-check all borders):** each of the 34 maps from 2000 BCE to 1783 was compared with Cliopatria for its year (`hb-vs-clio`-style overlap of every shape with the polities beneath it). historical-basemaps stays the base. Where it leaves a state out, draws it inside a neighbour or keeps it after it fell, the state is redrawn from Cliopatria's outline (`BEFORE_1800` in `build-data.mjs`; `from` limits each change to the shapes being fixed, so neighbours keep their borders), and the renames, rulers and notes that go with them are in `data-corrections.json`. Land it leaves blank or unnamed gets the state Cliopatria draws there, where one ruled (`fill`). Biggest fixes: the Delhi Sultanate in 1400 (drawn at its 1330s size over the Bahmani Sultanate, Bengal, Jaunpur, Sindh and Vijayanagara), Jin China in 1200 (given to the Song), Visigothic Spain in 700, the Kushans (200) and Hephthalites (500), Seljuk Anatolia in 1100 and 1200, Assyria in 700 BCE, the Zhou states before 221 BCE, Mongolia and the Kazakh steppe around 1500 and the Inca Empire in 1492 (with the peoples it ruled marked as its subjects). Cliopatria is wrong in places too (Sindh in 900, the Golden Horde after 1502, Yemen in 600), so every change was checked against the history first. A second pass (Oct 27) fixed what the first missed, including the pre-1800 daily-puzzle shapes listed in #93: the leftover "Chagatai Khanate" and the Mongol state or Ming drawn over Manchuria from 1400 to 1600 (now "Jurchens and Amur peoples"), 1600's "central Asian khanates" over Mongolia and Siberia, the Fatimids' old outline across North Africa in 1100 and 1200, Sri Lanka under the Cholas in 1100 and 1200, Bukhara drawn as Khiva (1530–1715), Kashmir inside the Samanids (900) and Karakhanids (1100), Bactria south of the Hindu Kush (200 BCE), Babylonia in Iran (1500 BCE), and the Americas in 1783 (Rupert's Land, the viceroyalties and the Guianas from OpenHistoricalMap's outlines for that year) and the Caribbean from 1650 to 1783.
- **Every spot belongs to one shape (Oct 20):** the sources often draw territories on top of each other (1492 had ~2,900 overlapping pairs, 1600–1815 ~400 each), and the map strokes every outline, so hidden outlines showed as stray dotted lines through other shapes. `scripts/lib/overlaps.mjs` cuts them apart: where two shapes overlap, the smaller keeps the shared ground and the larger gets a hole, so every territory stays visible. Area and label point follow the cut shape. `npm run data` does this before coloring; `node scripts/fix-overlaps.mjs` redoes it on the existing files (and recolours). One small pair in Australia (Wailwan / Kamilaroi, 1600–1800) is left as the clipping fails there.
- **Coasts reach the sea (Oct 22):** the source's outlines often stop short of the real coastline, which left blank ("not mapped") strips between shapes and the sea on every map. `scripts/lib/gaps.mjs` gives each strip to the shape beside it, shared out by nearest where it borders several (unnamed shapes count too, so no country runs along a coast in front of one). It fills thin strips only (area / perimeter up to 10 km, about 20 km wide), since wider blank land can be a real gap in the source, except around Australia, whose coarse coastline needs every strip filled. Islands no shape touches stay "not mapped". It runs after colouring; a shape that ends up beside a same-coloured neighbour gets a free colour. Missing land that shares an edge with a shape is always left to this fill (marked `j` in `build-data.mjs`, filled whatever its width): the island step used to add it to today's country as a separate piece, which drew the old coastline through the country and ran Libya along Egypt's coast. Real islands are still added to their country there. Thin unnamed wedges between neighbours go whole to the neighbour they share most border with, and a merge that would lose any of a shape's own ground is skipped.
- **One color per country for life (Oct 20):** every country, empire or people keeps one color from the first era it appears in to the last (`scripts/lib/colors.mjs`). All 49 eras are colored together (DSATUR), so two owners that ever border each other never share a color; the eight palette colors fit with no clashes. Renames that add words fold into one country ("United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland" = "United Kingdom", "Empire of Brazil" = "Brazil") unless the two ever border, and so do countries that carry on under a new name from one era to the next (German Empire → Germany, Russian Empire → USSR → Russia, Ottoman Empire → Türkiye: each one's label point inside the other's land, or the main successor), unless two of them are ever on the map together; a ruler named without a shape of its own (1914 "Russia", 1945 "USA") is read as its homeland. Colonies take their ruler's color, so a place changes color only when it changes hands. `npm run data` does this at the end; `node scripts/recolor-eras.mjs` redoes it on the existing files (`--check` reports).
- **Borders sweep into each new era (Oct 22):** changing era (Play, ◀ ▶, the slider, typing a year) moves borders to their new place over 2 seconds (`src/components/map/sweep.ts`). A country that gains land spreads into it from its old border; a new country sweeps in from one side (its border with whoever carries on the country it takes most land from, else its longest border), never from every edge at once. **Only the border moves:** the frame is the new era's normal tiles with the old era's picture clipped to the land the border hasn't reached, plus the moving border line in the map's own style, so colors, solid/dashed lines and lakes never change, and the end is exactly the new era's map. Who carries on whom uses the colors from `scripts/lib/colors.mjs` (decided on the whole world, not the view). Names stay on, follow their country and switch halfway when it is renamed; new countries are named from halfway. Moving the map mid-sweep or "reduce motion" shows the new era straight away; Play waits for each sweep. (A first slide, #47–#51, was removed in #54 because colors, dotted lines and names changed during it; this version keeps all of them.) **Lakes are the same on every era:** big lakes (`scripts/build-lakes.mjs` → `public/data/lakes.json`, natural lakes of 1,000 km² or more from Natural Earth) are drawn as water on top of every era's tiles, since the sources disagree about which lakes they cut out.
- **The map lives only on `/map`.** The homepage leads to games, the map and information pages. Since Oct 23 (Anthony picked mockup B of three): headline, then today's game beside **yesterday's matchup** (the trickiest pair from yesterday's real game, map size vs real size, picked in the browser because the puzzle changes at each player's midnight), the map card, then the Best streaks board and a one-line-per-page list. All over a slowly turning globe you can't drag. About 2,850px tall on a phone (was 6,450). Fully static: no per-request parts.
- **Daily puzzles** are picked by a seeded shuffle of the date, so everyone gets the same five. The leaderboard re-marks answers on the server.
- **Daily game screens (Oct 21–22, Anthony's calls):** a 5-pill progress bar and, for players with a streak, a "N-day streak" pill above it. Each round reveals how the two compare on a web map as a dashed outline and how they really compare as a solid shape (one scale per view, so the legend says "compare", Oct 23); Antarctica's map drawing is its own outline blown up to its web-map area (~24×), not the Mercator strip (Anthony, Oct 23), then shows a verdict panel (soft green when right, soft red when wrong, since Oct 23, Anthony approved) and "Next round". On laptops the /daily header is compact and, on screens under 820px tall, the tiles are 5:4, so both tiles fit on the first screen. **Results order:** score with a fun title (5 "Emperor!", 4 "Empire builder!", 3 "Regional power", 0–2 "The map fooled you"), the five result tiles, then **"Get on the leaderboard" first**: Join opens a pop-up with clear steps (Oct 23, Anthony picked it after a friend missed the old inline second button): step 1 the 13+ box and a gold "Continue with email or Google", step 2 pick a nickname, then "You're #N today!" with the top three (a bottom sheet on phones, a centred box on laptops; `JoinDialog.tsx`, logic in `useJoin.ts`). The pop-up steps aside while Clerk's sign-in window is open and reopens at step 2 after sign-in, including after Google's page change (a `join-flow` flag in sessionStorage, 30 minutes), then the streak / today's rank / all-time cards, one compact share row (Share · Picture · Challenge), the answers ("Compare sizes on the map") and a big gold "Play more rounds for fun". Signing in from /daily pulls today's result from the account straight away, so a player who already played lands on their results without a refresh or a replay. Drawn icons (`src/components/site/Icons.tsx`) are used instead of emoji where they carry meaning. Since Oct 27 (#165), the results card is 'Save your streak on every device' (one sign-in step, no nickname). The rank card is gone. JoinDialog's nickname steps are used by endless mode only. **Oct 22 follow-ups:** every historical place has a "what it was / today" note (`src/content/daily-context.ts`; new pool shapes need one too); "Play more rounds for fun" scrolls back to the top of the new round; sounds are on by default (players who turn them off stay off); the reveal's second line uses the same 15% threshold as the cards' "Map: 1.2× too big" chips.
- **Leaderboard model (Oct 27, Anthony's call, #165):** one public board, endless Best streaks. Daily scores are private: a public daily board rewards cheating and early birds and puts off most players. Old daily, weekly and all-time data stays in Redis, hidden.
- **Milestones (Oct 27, #166):** daily streak banners at 3/7/14/30/50/100/365; endless flame tiers at 10 (gold) and 25 (blue); homepage boxes are Streak + Perfect days, or Today N/5 + Streak.
- **Growing the puzzle pool (Oct 20):** new shapes are added in `scripts/build-daily.mjs` and get a `since` date two days after the build; a dated puzzle only uses shapes in the pool that day, so past puzzles (and today's in every time zone) never change and the server keeps marking them correctly. Never edit or remove a shape whose `since` date has passed (or that has none). Leave out states the map data shows outside the years they existed. Pool: 337 shapes (Oct 20). **Since Oct 23 the build freezes the pool:** shapes already in it keep their entry exactly (areas, latitude, `since`, order) and only their outlines are redrawn, so map-data fixes can never change a puzzle already played; a shape whose land changed by more than 2% in the map data keeps its old outline too, so the picture always matches its area. Outline detail comes from the main landmass (the same cluster the game frames), so countries with far-off islands (France, the Netherlands) are drawn as sharply as the rest. Pool: 338 shapes.
- **Weekly review (Mondays 8:47 ET, scheduled task):** checks the news for border and dispute changes (two reliable sources before editing), new historical findings, upstream map-data releases, and adds ~20 more puzzle shapes. Pool growth merges after CI; history corrections and map-data rebuilds wait for Anthony's OK.
- **Leaderboard sign-up (Oct 19):** an email and a nickname are required to join. Emails are stored privately in Redis (`lb2:emails`), never returned by the API, and each inbox gets one player. Since Oct 19 the email is verified by signing in with Clerk (see below).
- **Fresh start (Oct 19):** when verified sign-in went live, the leaderboard was reset: Redis keys moved from `lb:` to `lb2:` (old data left unused), and browsers' saved progress and player moved to `atlas-daily-v2` / `atlas-player-v2`. Everyone starts again by signing in.
- **Verified sign-in with Clerk (Oct 19):** to post a score you sign in with an email code or Google (Clerk), so nobody can play under an email they don't own, and you keep one nickname on every device and browser. Clerk only proves the email: the player, nickname, scores and reminders stay in our Redis, found by the verified email (`lb2:email-owners`) and tied to the Clerk account (`lb2:accounts`). A player who joined before sign-in keeps their spot by signing in with the same email. **Switching away later** (e.g. our own email codes via Resend once there's a domain) only means replacing `verifiedUser()` in `src/lib/account.ts`; everyone signs in once more and lands on the same player. Keys set Oct 19 (development keys). **Caveat:** Clerk's production mode needs a domain we own; on `scale-shift.vercel.app` it runs in development mode (a small badge, and Clerk caps development sign-ups at 100), fine until the domain is decided.
- **Navigation (Oct 23):** the phone menu leads with the game (Daily challenge, Leaderboard, then Map). The desktop header marks the current section (era pages count as Eras). The phone bottom bar is hidden on /daily, /contact, /privacy, /terms and /unsubscribed. On /leaderboard and /eras* it offers "Play today's game" (gold) until today is played, otherwise "Explore the real-size map". Icons are drawn SVGs in `src/components/site/Icons.tsx`, never emoji (except the 🟩🟥 copy-paste share grid).
- **Privacy, terms and age (Oct 23, Anthony's calls):** the contact address is `borderdrift.team@gmail.com` (a shared Gmail, forwarded to Anthony; move to a domain address once borderdrift.com is bought). `/privacy` and `/terms` copy lives in `src/content/legal.ts`; keep it true to the code whenever data handling changes. An "I'm 13 or older" checkbox is required before any sign-in or email join (`AgeCheck.tsx`, remembered in `atlas-13plus`); a lawyer may prefer a neutral birth-year question before ads. "Delete my data" on /leaderboard removes the player from Redis (`deletePlayer`) and deletes the Clerk account. The old `lb:` keys from before Oct 19 should be purged in the Upstash console so the policy holds.
- **Measuring who comes back (Oct 24, Anthony's call after a retention review):** Vercel Analytics on Hobby has no custom events, so the game counts itself, anonymously. On finishing the daily, the browser sends `{date, score, first}` (`first` = days since it first finished a daily) to `POST /api/plays`; the server only increments per-day counters in Redis (`pl:day:<date>`: `n`, `s0`…`s5`, `f0`…`f30`, `fx`). No id, cookie or address is stored, so no consent banner. Day-1 return = count(D+1, `f1`) ÷ count(D, `f0`). `npm run plays` prints it. The results screen shows "Better than N% of players today" from these counts (30+ plays, only when N ≥ 30%; a perfect round "Top N%" up to 50%), "If you join #N" only once 20+ have joined, otherwise days played, plus a "Your scores" chart from the second day. The share text carries the streak and a dare. **Retention plan, in order:** reminder emails (after the domain), streak freeze, archive of past days, Home Screen nudge then web push, endless mode. Judge with 14 days of numbers: D1 target 25–35%, D7 10–15% (our assumptions, not published benchmarks).

Every reminder must carry the signed one-click unsubscribe link (`unsubscribeToken`). Sending is not built yet: it needs a real domain, Resend and `UNSUBSCRIBE_SECRET` in Vercel.
- **Nickname filter:** `src/lib/name-filter.ts` reads names several ways (l33t, q-for-g, lookalike letters, repeats, separators). Boards hide any saved name that fails it, so adding a word removes a live bad name on the next deploy.
- **Storage:** form messages and leaderboard scores need Vercel settings to persist (see the setup log).

## Known issues and limits
- Borders before about 1650 are approximate (the source rates most borders precise from 1650; era labels say "Mostly precise/approximate" because an era's rating is an average, Oct 23); snapshots are 50–200 years apart in ancient times.
- Still different from CShapes after the 1900+ work (Oct 25), mostly frontiers that were not yet agreed: Tibet's extent (1914–1945), Ecuador–Peru before 1938, the Chaco, Egypt–Libya before 1925 (we use the 1925 line), Kenya–Ethiopia in 1900, the north of the British Cameroons (run with Nigeria), Uganda's Rudolf Province (Kenyan in our 1920), and Yemen–Aden (by province). Some 1900 colonies are drawn from today's provinces and marked "shown approximately" (Uganda's Eastern Province, the Nigerian protectorates, Barotseland, Wadai). Japanese-occupied China in 1938 is not shown. Where CShapes keeps an older legal border although control had changed (Poland's east in 1920, Hungary's north in 1945), our maps show the line held on the ground. Turkey in 1920 is drawn within its later Lausanne borders, without the Greek-held zones around Smyrna and in Eastern Thrace.
- 1800–1960 (Oct 25): Cliopatria and OpenHistoricalMap map states, not peoples, so land no state held is "not mapped" instead of shown as the Indigenous nations who lived there (the old source drew peoples, but Evan asked not to use it here). A later fix could add nations from a public-domain source such as the US Royce land-cession maps. In India the states the East India Company protected (Hyderabad, Mysore, Travancore) are drawn with it, as both sources do; Cutch is drawn approximately.
- 1800 and 1815 (open questions from PR #137, Oct 26): the 1815 map draws Suriname, Curaçao, Martinique and Guadeloupe under their legal owners, with a note, although Britain held them until 1816 (Java and the other Dutch posts in the East Indies are drawn British). OpenHistoricalMap's Banjar (1801–1830) covers all of southern and eastern Borneo, and its Portuguese Timor covers most of Flores.
- 1900–1960 (Oct 25): Spain is drawn whole in 1938, as on the reviewed map (Cliopatria has the civil-war zones); the Byelorussian SSR of 1920 and Ifni are drawn approximately; Arabia's Empty Quarter and northern deserts are "not mapped". The present-day map follows Natural Earth's de facto Western Sahara (the land Morocco administers is drawn as Morocco); the 1900–1960 maps draw the Spanish Sahara to 27°40′N instead.
- Antarctic claim areas exclude the floating ice shelves.
- Searchable places are countries, US states and historical states. The v1 site also had Canadian and other provinces, continents and "all claims" realms; bringing those back needs data-pipeline work.
- Forms don't keep anything on Vercel until their settings are added (see below).
- Sign-in (Clerk) runs in development mode on scale-shift.vercel.app (a small badge and a 100-sign-up cap) until the domain is bought.
- Vercel's Hobby plan is for non-commercial use. Keep the repo public, or move to Pro, if either owner's merges stop deploying.
- Selling or showing ads (checked Oct 25, Evan): the data from 1800 to 1960 allows commercial use with credit. Cliopatria and HistoGIS are CC BY 4.0, OpenHistoricalMap is CC0 apart from some 630 border lines under CC BY 4.0 and 10 under MIT (their makers are credited in `public/data/LICENSE.txt`), and Natural Earth is public domain. CShapes is never shipped. Before charging money: move from Vercel Hobby (non-commercial only) to Pro, drop the "non-commercial" wording (footer, /about, terms), and decide on historical-basemaps, which still draws the maps from 2000 BCE to 1783 and the daily puzzle's history shapes. Its GPL-3.0 licence allows commercial use as long as the data stays GPL with its source open, but its author turned down a request for commercial use ("Sorry, no commercial use. This is GPL for a reason.", aourednik/historical-basemaps issue #76).

## How we work (agreed Oct 22)
- **One task at a time.** The queue lives in issue #40. Claude builds one task end to end, then a separate **reviewer agent** checks it independently (bugs, accuracy, Anthony's exact words, a phone-size playthrough). Facts get a second **fact-check agent** with two sources each. Anthony confirms the merge, and only then does the next task start.
