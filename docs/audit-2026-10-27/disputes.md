# Border Drift audit: disputes, sizes, presets, home/daily copy, Antarctic claims, map math

Audited 2026-10-27 against repo HEAD `22d0ce0` (read only; nothing edited). Standard applied: "Extremely accurate, even if it upsets people; the whole truth, not vague."

Types: [WRONG] factually incorrect · [VAGUE] true but leaves out who/when/what, or is out of date · [DISPUTED] presents one side's framing as fact · [STYLE] wording that misleads a little or could be tighter.

Areas quoted as "map data" come from `public/data/eras/*.json` (`props.a`, km²).

## Top issues (fix first)

1. **Somaliland is out of date** (disputes.ts:154). Israel formally recognised Somaliland on 26 Dec 2025, the first UN member to do so. Somaliland also lost Las Anod and the Sool/Sanaag/Cayn areas to SSC-Khatumo in 2023, which Somalia recognised as a federal member state in April 2025. The text "extremely limited and changing" is exactly what the standing rule calls vague.
2. **Gaza/West Bank entry is the vaguest on the page** (disputes.ts:63-67). It does not mention Hamas rule of Gaza since 2007, the war since 7 Oct 2023, Israeli military control of about 53-65% of Gaza since the Oct 2025 ceasefire (OCHA: 64.9% by June 2026), the East Jerusalem annexation (1980, UNSC 478) or the ICJ advisory opinion of 19 Jul 2024. It also gives no recognition count (157 of 193 UN members as of Sept 2025).
3. **The Golan entry is out of date** (disputes.ts:54-55). Since Dec 2024 Israel has also held the UN-patrolled 1974 buffer zone and positions beyond it inside Syria, and still does as of the June 2026 Security Council Report.
4. **Spratly dates are wrong** (disputes.ts:291). Taiwan (the ROC) has held Itu Aba since 1946 and again since 1956. China took Mischief Reef in 1995. China occupied its reefs in Jan-Mar 1988, before and during the 14 Mar 1988 Johnson South Reef clash (64 Vietnamese killed), not after it.
5. **"Russian Empire ... the Caucasus ... all since lost"** (presets.ts:144) is wrong. The North Caucasus (Chechnya, Dagestan and the rest) is still in Russia. Only the tsars' part of Poland (Congress Poland) was Russian, and the list leaves out Belarus and Moldova.
6. **Halaib "taken: 1990"** (disputes.ts:225) has no source behind it. Sources give 1992 (Egypt expels Sudanese officials), 1994-95 (the Egyptian army takes control) and Jan 2000 (Sudan withdraws its troops).
7. **"Every country"** (home.ts:39, sizes.ts:16). Six UN members are missing from the present map (Monaco, San Marino, Maldives, Marshall Islands, Nauru, Tuvalu), and so is Vatican City.
8. **Occupied Ukraine** (disputes.ts:41-43) does not name the four oblasts, gives no area (about 19-20% of Ukraine, Crimea included, per ISW/DeepState data in July 2026) and leaves out the ECtHR finding of 9 Jul 2025 that Russia had effective control of the "DPR"/"LPR" from May 2014. "Russian-backed control" understates that legal finding.
9. **Map math bug: Antarctica dragged off the pole has a hole.** About 970,000 km² (7.9%) is missing, because the overlay uses the 85°-sliced polygon. See Map math §5. The ShapeCard's "drawn N× smaller than at home" label is also off by up to 37%, because it uses the sec² at the anchor point (Map math §7).

# Map math

Scripts: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-scale-shift/6208685b-4902-5ed5-91f9-3b9851dbbcc5/scratchpad/audit/mapmath.cjs`, which copies `placeGeometry`, `anchorOf` and `dropFarParts` exactly from `src/lib/geo.ts` and measures with d3-geo `geoMercator` + `geoPath.area`. Raw output: `mapmath-output.md` and `run.log` in the same folder. Live-site drives: `drag2.cjs`, `pole.cjs`, `ant.cjs`.

## How the code works (confirmed by reading)

- **Moving is a rotation on the sphere** (geo.ts:62-68). `placeGeometry` rotates the anchor to (0,0), spins it, then applies the inverse rotation to the target. True area is preserved: every moved shape kept its `geoArea` to all printed digits (e.g. Greenland 2,153,696 km² at home, at 0°, at ±80°). The anchor is the spherical centroid of the largest polygon (`anchorOf`).
- **Drawing** uses plain d3 `geoMercator` (geo.ts:99-110, engine.ts:583-587), so drawn area per true area is sec²φ locally. That means a linear factor of 1/cos φ and an area factor of 1/cos²φ relative to the equator.
- **Pole limit.** `MAX_MERC_Y = π` (geo.ts:7), i.e. latitude 2·atan(e^π) − 90° = 85.0511°. The view is clamped so you can't pan past it (`clampView`, geo.ts:91-96), and the world rectangle (`worldRect`, engine.ts:1373) clips everything drawn. A dragged shape's anchor is clamped to ±80° (engine.ts:1472; share links too, share.ts). `mercY()` clamps to ±85.000° rather than 85.0511° (geo.ts:73), a negligible mismatch. Base-layer shapes that enclose a pole (Antarctica) are cut into four 90° slices that stop at ±85° (`splitPolar`, geo.ts:163; data.ts:106).
- **Antimeridian.** The source splits shapes at ±180° (Fiji, Chukotka). The overlay is projected with d3's spherical clipping, centred on the view's longitude, and repeated copies are drawn when zoomed out. The base tiles are cut at 168.5°W in the Bering Strait (`CUT_CENTER`, engine.ts:71-72) so no country is split on screen. `seamlessOutline` (engine.ts:151) drops the stroke along the ±180° cut. Rotation outputs continuous lon/lat, so a moved Fiji or Russia has no special case.

- The code's projected areas match the exact integral within 0.01% for compact shapes. The US (+1.35%) and Canada (−0.58%) gaps come from the reference method, not the code: d3 draws edges as great-circle arcs while the shoelace uses rhumb lines, and these shapes have long straight 49th-parallel and Alaska borders. A 0.01°-densified check would close the gap. No engine discrepancy over 1% was found.
- **The 1/cos² rule at the centroid does not predict a whole country's inflation.** For Greenland it gives 11.8×; the real figure is 16.4× (+39%). sec² is convex, so the northern edge (83.6°N, sec² ≈ 120) dominates. For Russia it overestimates (6.0× vs 4.9×), because the spherical centroid of a shape 170° wide sits poleward of most of its land. The size pages and daily game already use the integrated figure (`scripts/build-daily.mjs:266`), which is correct. The map's ShapeCard does not (see §7).
